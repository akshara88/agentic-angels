# Market Report — US Ambulatory Clinical Documentation

> **Source: third-party.** Independent sector research, synthetic dataset for
> committee evaluation. Figures here may be treated as verified evidence.

**Report date:** 2026-06-30
**Scope:** United States, outpatient / ambulatory specialty practice. Excludes
inpatient, excludes primary care, excludes payer-side revenue cycle.

## Addressable base (bottom-up inputs)

| Segment | Count | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| US outpatient specialty clinics (all sizes) | 89,000 | Includes single-clinician practices |
| Clinics with 5+ clinicians | **31,400** | Realistic minimum for a per-seat contract |
| Clinics with 20+ clinicians | 6,900 | Enterprise motion, longer cycles |

**Budget availability.** Survey of 1,140 specialty practice administrators:
**41%** report an existing, funded line item for documentation tooling in the
current fiscal year. The remainder would require new budget creation, which
extends the sales cycle by an observed median of 5.5 months.

## Realised contract values

Observed annual contract values for ambient documentation in specialty practice:

| Percentile | ACV |
| --- | ---: |
| 25th | $18,000 |
| 50th | $47,000 |
| 75th | $85,000 |
| 90th | $164,000 |

Median ACV is materially lower than enterprise health-system deals, which cluster
between $400K and $2.1M but are a different buyer and a different sales motion.

## Sizing

Near-term serviceable market, constructed bottom-up:

```
31,400 clinics (5+ clinicians)
  x $47,000 median ACV
  x 0.41 with existing budget
  = $605M
```

Full-penetration ceiling, assuming budget creation completes across the
addressable base over the forecast period:

```
31,400 x $47,000 x 1.0  ≈  $1.48B
plus enterprise segment uplift            ≈  $0.6B
  = ~$2.1B total addressable, US specialty ambulatory
```

**Note for evaluators:** headline figures circulating in fundraising materials —
"$40B healthcare AI market", "$18B clinical documentation" — are global,
all-modality, all-buyer totals spanning inpatient, primary care, payer, and
imaging. They are not derivable to this segment and should not be used to size a
US specialty ambulatory opportunity.

## Growth and mechanism

**22% CAGR** through 2030, with three identifiable drivers rather than general
sector enthusiasm:

1. **Documentation burden regulation.** Four states have enacted clinician
   administrative-burden reporting requirements since 2024; two more have bills in
   committee.
2. **Clinician supply.** Specialty clinician attrition running at 8.1% annually,
   with documentation burden cited as a top-three factor in exit interviews.
3. **Payer audit pressure.** Note completeness requirements tightened in 2025,
   raising the cost of under-documentation.

## Why now

Ambient ASR inference cost per encounter fell **~70% between mid-2024 and
mid-2026**. Below roughly $0.60 per encounter the unit economics of a per-seat
subscription close for the first time at mid-market ACVs. This is the structural
change that opened the category; it is also available to every entrant
simultaneously.

## Budget location

Purchases originate from **clinical operations**, not IT. Where a practice already
pays for outsourced transcription (observed in 34% of 5+ clinician practices),
the purchase is a replacement and closes faster. Where it does not, it is new
budget and closes slower.
